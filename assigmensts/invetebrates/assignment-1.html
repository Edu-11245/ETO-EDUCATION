The history of animal classification

Aristotle
"Father of Zoology" (384–322 BC)		John Ray
(1627–1705)		Carolus Linnaeus
(1707–1778)
  • Greek philosopher; first to classify animals scientifically (~500 kinds), book Historia Animalium.
  • Basis: presence of red blood, habitat, mode of reproduction.
  • Two main groups:
    Enaima (red-blooded): viviparous quadrupeds, birds, oviparous quadrupeds, fishes, whales.
    Anaima (bloodless): cephalopods, crustaceans, insects, shelled animals (molluscs, echinoderms).
  • English naturalist.
  • Introduced the species concept as the unit of classification: individuals that produce similar offspring.
  • Classified animals by structural features (e.g. hoofed vs. clawed mammals; number of toes).
  • Works: Synopsis Methodica Animalium Quadrupedum, Historia Insectorum.
  • Swedish botanist; "Father of modern taxonomy".
  • Book Systema Naturae (1735; 10th ed. 1758 is the starting point of zoological nomenclature).
  • Introduced binomial nomenclature (genus + species, in Latin), e.g. Homo sapiens.
  • Hierarchy: Kingdom → Class → Order → Genus → Species (Phylum and Family added later).
  • Divided animals into 6 classes: Mammalia, Aves, Amphibia, Pisces, Insecta, Vermes.
