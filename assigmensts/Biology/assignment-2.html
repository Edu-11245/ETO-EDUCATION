Define the following terms

Scientific TermDefinition
AnaimaAristotle's group of animals without red blood (invertebrates).
EnaimaAristotle's group of animals with red blood (vertebrates).
HerbivorousAnimals that feed only on plants (e.g. cow, rabbit).
CarnivorousAnimals that feed on the flesh of other animals (e.g. lion, shark).
SpeciesA group of similar individuals that can interbreed in nature and produce fertile offspring, and are reproductively isolated from other groups.
Binomial nomenclatureLinnaeus' two-name system for naming organisms: the genus name (capital first letter) followed by the species name (small letters), in Latin, italic or underlined, e.g. Homo sapiens.
SymmetryThe arrangement of body parts around a central point or axis so that the body can be divided into similar halves (types: asymmetry, spherical, radial, bilateral).
DiploblasticAnimals whose body develops from two germ layers: ectoderm and endoderm, with a jelly-like mesoglea between them (e.g. Cnidaria).
TriploblasticAnimals whose body develops from three germ layers: ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm.
CoelomA true body cavity between the body wall and the digestive tract, completely lined by mesoderm (peritoneum).
AcoelomataAnimals without a body cavity; the space is filled with mesenchyme (e.g. flatworms).
PseudocoelomataAnimals with a false body cavity, not completely lined by mesoderm (e.g. roundworms/nematodes).
CoelomataAnimals with a true coelom (e.g. annelids, molluscs, arthropods, vertebrates).
Bisexual / hermaphroditic / monoeciousAn individual that has both male and female reproductive organs (e.g. earthworm, tapeworm).
Unisexual / gonochoristic / dioeciousSexes are separate: each individual is either male or female (e.g. Ascaris, human).
Metamerism or SegmentationDivision of the body along its long axis into a series of similar repeated parts.
Segment / somite / metamereEach one of the repeated similar parts into which the body is divided in metamerism (e.g. a ring of the earthworm).
