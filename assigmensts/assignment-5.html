Define the following terms

Scientific TermDefinition
ParazoaSub-kingdom of multicellular animals at the cellular grade of organization: no true tissues or organs (the sponges).
PoriferaPhylum of sponges ("pore-bearers"): mostly marine, sessile animals whose body is perforated by pores and a canal system.
SpongillidaeFamily of freshwater sponges, e.g. Spongilla.
OstiaMinute inhalant pores in the body wall through which water enters the sponge.
Spongocoel / atrium / paragastric cavityThe central cavity of the sponge that receives water from the canals and opens to the outside by the osculum.
OsculumThe large exhalant opening at the top of the sponge through which water leaves.
ChoanocytesCollar cells: flagellated cells with a collar, lining the spongocoel, radial canals or flagellated chambers; they create the water current and trap food.
GemmulesInternal buds for asexual reproduction in freshwater sponges; resistant to harsh conditions and contain archaeocytes.
AmphiblastulaFree-swimming larva of calcareous sponges: hollow, half flagellated small cells and half large granular cells.
ParenchymulaSolid, free-swimming larva of some sponges: outer flagellated cells and an inner cell mass.
CnidocytesStinging cells of Cnidaria containing a nematocyst, used for defense and capturing prey.
Gastrovascular cavity / coelenteron / enteronThe central sac-like cavity of Cnidaria with a single opening (mouth); used for digestion and distribution of food.
CoelomataAnimals possessing a true coelom lined by mesoderm.
PlanulaFree-swimming, ciliated, oval larva of Cnidaria.
PolymorphismOccurrence of two or more different forms (e.g. polyp and medusa) within the same species, with division of labour.
Metagenesis (alternation of generation)Life cycle alternating between an asexual generation (polyp, reproduces by budding) and a sexual generation (medusa, produces gametes), e.g. Obelia.
